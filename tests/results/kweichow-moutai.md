## Key analytical findings

1. 2025 revenue and earnings declined; 2026 YTD is roughly flat. High historical ROE does not imply the former growth rate can continue.
2. Channel mix is changing sharply toward direct sales. This can improve consumer visibility and value capture but also changes dealer incentives and reported channel signals.
3. The company explicitly described the old pattern of channel push → price fluctuation → negative market feedback and said it chose not to optimize only for headline targets. This is a strong empirical case for adding Goodhart's Law to the model set.
4. Headline operating cash flow growth in 2026 is materially affected by finance-company interbank deposit movements; evidence discipline prevents treating it as pure liquor-business cash generation.
5. Contract liabilities fell sharply year-on-year by 2026Q3. This deserves monitoring but is not independently sufficient to infer end-demand collapse because selling mechanics are changing.
6. Moat remains strong; the security question is now more about sustainable growth, terminal economics, payout/capital allocation and valuation.

## Model-set implication

**Goodhart's Law** receives a strong positive replacement-candidate signal.

Reason: current models can describe incentives (044), feedback (072), system traps (077), and observability (079), but none states the specific mechanism that once a sales/volume/KPI metric becomes the target, participants optimize the metric at the expense of true consumer demand and channel health.

This is incremental rather than cosmetic.
